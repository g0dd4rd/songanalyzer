🎼 Harmonic Progression & Key Analyzer
🎓 Student Guide • Harmonic Progression & Key Analyzer

What it is: Deconstructs chord progressions, calculates constituent note pitches, determines the home key center using diatonic fit and tonic confidence, and performs Roman numeral analysis with secondary dominant and chromatic substitution detection.

Why use it: Understand why chord progressions sound good, decode the key of songs by ear, discover cadences (like jazz ii - V - I or the Andalusian cadence), and learn how tension resolves to stability.

How to use it:
  1. Pick a preset from the -- Select Preset -- dropdown (e.g. Jazz ii - V - I, Pop I - V - vi - IV, Coltrane Changes) or type chords into the input field (e.g. Dm7 G7 Cmaj7 A7b9).
  2. Click Analyze (or press Enter) to view the chord notes table, Roman numeral scale degrees, and the Key Center: recommendations.
  3. Click ▶ Play Progression (with optional Loop) to hear polyphonic electric piano voicings with guide-tone voice leading.
  4. Click 📖 Chord Notation Guide to view all supported chord symbols (triads, 7ths, altered dominants, slash chords).
💡 Student Pro-Tip: Roman numerals tell you a chord's functional role in the key: uppercase (I, IV, V) are major, lowercase (ii, iii, vi) are minor. Notice how a dominant 7th chord (V7) contains a tritone that resolves outward/inward by half-step to the tonic (I).

🪐 Base-12 Modal Interchange & Mood Engine
🎓 Student Guide • Base-12 Modal Interchange & Mood Engine

What it is: Mathematically projects a chord progression (written as scale degrees like 1, 6, 4, 5 or 2, 5, 1) across all 7 parallel diatonic modes (from brightest Lydian to darkest Locrian) using chromatic pitch-class offsets to borrow chords.

Why use it: Transform the emotional mood of any song without rewriting its core melody. Borrowing chords from parallel modes (like minor iv in a major key) adds cinematic color and nostalgic lift.

How to use it:
  1. Enter your root tonic into Tonic (e.g. C) and scale degrees into Degrees (e.g. 1, 6, 4, 5 or 2, 5, 1).
  2. Click Transform Across Modes to compute the chords across all 7 parallel modes.
  3. Explore the generated mode cards: each displays its specific mood tag (e.g., Mystical / Ethereal, Heroic / Optimistic, Melancholic Jazz, Flamenco / Dark Spanish).
  4. Click ▶ Play Mode on any card to hear that specific modal progression played in real time.
💡 Student Pro-Tip: Modes are arranged on a brightness spectrum by number of sharps/flats: Lydian (brightest, ♯4) → Ionian (standard major) → Mixolydian (bluesy ♭7) → Dorian (jazzy minor, ♮6) → Aeolian (natural minor) → Phrygian (dark Spanish, ♭2) → Locrian (darkest, ♭5).

👁 HTML5 Canvas Visualizer
🎓 Student Guide • HTML5 Canvas Visualizer

What it is: A real-time geometric visualizer with three selectable perspectives: the 12-tone Chromatic Circle (Clock (Z12 Wheel)), the Piano Keyboard, and an interactive multi-string Fretboard (with 6-String Guitar and 4-String Bass modes).

Why use it: Music theory is geometry! Seeing chords mapped onto the chromatic circle reveals symmetrical harmonies (diminished 7ths form squares, augmented chords form equilateral triangles). The fretboard maps exact fingerings across strings.

How to use it:
  1. Use the top switcher buttons to toggle between Clock (Z12 Wheel), Piano Keyboard, and Fretboard.
  2. When Fretboard is selected, choose your instrument from the dropdown (6-String Guitar (E A D G B E) or 4-String Bass (E A D G)).
  3. Play any progression in the Analyzer, Jam Deck, or Drills — the canvas updates in real time: root notes glow in gold, while chord tones appear in cyan.
💡 Student Pro-Tip: Notes directly opposite each other on the circle are 6 semitones apart (a Tritone). In a G7 chord (G-B-D-F), the B and F form a tritone line that collapses inward to C and E when resolving to C Major.
🃏 Musician's Jam Deck (Card Game)
🎓 Student Guide • Musician's Jam Deck (Card Game)

What it is: A creative flashcard generator that deals random chord cards from customizable card pools (Pure Wildcards or Diatonic + Spice) for songwriting and improvisation games.

Why use it: Busts writer's block, forces you to discover fresh chord combinations outside your comfort zone, and tests your ability to connect unrelated harmonies with smooth voice leading.

How to use it:
  1. Select how many cards you want using the card count dropdown (3 to 6 cards).
  2. Choose the card pool: Pure Wildcards (adventurous harmony) or Diatonic + Spice (harmonically grounded with secondary dominants).
  3. Click 🃏 Deal Cards to draw a new hand.
  4. Drag and drop cards to reorder them into a satisfying musical sequence.
  5. Click ▶ Play to hear your card progression looped.
  6. Click 💾 Save TXT to export your chord chart to your computer, or 🎼 Send to Analyzer to inspect its Roman numerals and key center!
  7. Click 🎲 New Jam Prompt to get specific practice challenges (e.g. guide-tone voice leading).
💡 Student Pro-Tip: Follow the Jam Prompt: strive for smooth voice leading between cards! Keep common notes between neighboring chords stationary, and move other notes by only a half-step or whole-step.

⏱ Metronome & Rhythm Studio
🎓 Student Guide • Metronome & Rhythm Studio

What it is: A high-precision, zero-drift Web Audio metronome with arbitrary time signatures (e.g. 4/4, 7/8, 16/15), subdivisions (quarter, 8ths, triplets, 16ths, quintuplets), polyrhythm clave generator, and an integrated ⏳ Practice Session Timer with automatic delta logging.

Why use it: Develop rock-solid internal timing, master odd meters and syncopation, and track exact practice minutes per exercise so you know where your practice time went.

How to use it:
  1. Set your meter in Meter: (metroTimeSigInput, e.g. 4/4, 7/8, 5/4) and pick a subdivision from metroSubdivision (e.g. Triplets, 16ths).
  2. Adjust tempo using the slider, Tempo: input, or -5, -1, +1, +5 buttons.
  3. Choose a sound from metroSound (Woodblock, Digital Click, or Spoken Count).
  4. Click ▶ Start Metronome — the metronome starts and the Practice Session Timer begins tracking elapsed exercise time automatically!
  5. When switching to a new drill, click ⏭ Next Exercise to log your completed delta and reset for the next study.
  6. In the Unicode Rhythm Generator, click 🎲 New Rhythm and ▶ Play Clave to practice playing over syncopated clave rhythms.
💡 Student Pro-Tip: For odd meters like 7/8, break the beats into asymmetric pulses: 2 + 2 + 3 or 3 + 2 + 2. Use the Spoken Count mode to hear verbal counting ("1 & 2 &" or "1 trip let") while playing!
