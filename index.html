🎓 Student Guide • Chromatic Strobe & Needle Tuner
🎯 How to Tune with the Gauge & Strobe:

Pluck your guitar or bass string firmly. The tuner will detect the pitch and display the note name (e.g. E2) and exact frequency in Hertz (Hz).

  1. If the needle is to the left (negative cents), your string is flat (tune up).
  2. If the needle is to the right (positive cents), your string is sharp (tune down).
  3. The Virtual Strobe Ring rotates counter-clockwise when flat and clockwise when sharp. When the strobe freezes stationary and the display glows emerald green, you have achieved perfect harmonic resonance (within ±3 cents)!
  4. Select alternate tunings (Drop D, DADGAD, Open G, 7-string, 5-string bass) from the dropdown—the Canvas Fretboard will instantly sync to your tuning!
💡 Student Pro-Tip: Always tune up to pitch, never down! If your string is sharp, tune below the note first, then slowly bring it up to pitch. This locks the string tension onto the machine head gears and prevents slipping while you play.

🎓 Student Guide • Harmonic Progression & Key Analyzer

What it is: Deconstructs chord progressions, calculates constituent note pitches, determines the home key center using diatonic fit and tonic confidence, and performs Roman numeral analysis with secondary dominant and chromatic substitution detection.

Why use it: Understand why chord progressions sound good, decode the key of songs by ear, discover cadences (like jazz ii - V - I or the Andalusian cadence), and learn how tension resolves to stability.

How to use it:
  1. Pick a preset from the -- Select Preset -- dropdown (e.g. Jazz ii - V - I, Pop I - V - vi - IV, Coltrane Changes) or type chords into the input field (e.g. Dm7 G7 Cmaj7 A7b9).
  2. Click Analyze (or press Enter) to view the chord notes table, Roman numeral scale degrees, and the Key Center: recommendations.
  3. Click ▶ Play Progression (with optional Loop) to hear polyphonic electric piano voicings with guide-tone voice leading.
  4. Click 📖 Chord Notation Guide to view all supported chord symbols (triads, 7ths, altered dominants, slash chords).
💡 Student Pro-Tip: Roman numerals tell you a chord's functional role in the key: uppercase (I, IV, V) are major, lowercase (ii, iii, vi) are minor. Notice how a dominant 7th chord (V7) contains a tritone that resolves outward/inward by half-step to the tonic (I).

🪐 Base-12 Modal Interchange & Mood Engine
🎓 Student Guide • Base-12 Modal Interchange & Mood Engine

What it is: Mathematically projects a chord progression (written as scale degrees like 1, 6, 4, 5 or 2, 5, 1) across all 7 parallel diatonic modes (from brightest Lydian to darkest Locrian) using chromatic pitch-class offsets to borrow chords.

Why use it: Transform the emotional mood of any song without rewriting its core melody. Borrowing chords from parallel modes (like minor iv in a major key) adds cinematic color and nostalgic lift.

How to use it:
  1. Enter your root tonic into Tonic (e.g. C) and scale degrees into Degrees (e.g. 1, 6, 4, 5 or 2, 5, 1).
  2. Click Transform Across Modes to compute the chords across all 7 parallel modes.
  3. Explore the generated mode cards: each displays its specific mood tag (e.g., Mystical / Ethereal, Heroic / Optimistic, Melancholic Jazz, Flamenco / Dark Spanish).
  4. Click ▶ Play Mode on any card to hear that specific modal progression played in real time.
💡 Student Pro-Tip: Modes are arranged on a brightness spectrum by number of sharps/flats: Lydian (brightest, ♯4) → Ionian (standard major) → Mixolydian (bluesy ♭7) → Dorian (jazzy minor, ♮6) → Aeolian (natural minor) → Phrygian (dark Spanish, ♭2) → Locrian (darkest, ♭5).

👁 HTML5 Canvas Visualizer
🎓 Student Guide • HTML5 Canvas Visualizer

What it is: A real-time geometric visualizer with three selectable perspectives: the 12-tone Chromatic Circle (Clock (Z12 Wheel) with multi-layer concentric groove tracks for Drums, Bass, Guitar, and Harmony Core), the Piano Keyboard, and an interactive multi-string Fretboard (with 6-String Guitar and 4-String Bass modes).

Why use it: Music theory is geometry! Seeing chords mapped onto the chromatic circle reveals symmetrical harmonies (diminished 7ths form squares, augmented chords form equilateral triangles). The fretboard maps exact fingerings across strings.

How to use it:
  1. Use the top switcher buttons to toggle between Clock (Z12 Wheel), Piano Keyboard, and Fretboard.
  2. In Clock (Z12 Wheel) mode, toggle concentric groove layers (🥁 Drums, 🎸 Bass, 🎼 Guitar, 🌟 Harmony) to see how rhythm locks with harmony in real time.
  3. When Fretboard is selected, choose your instrument from the dropdown (6-String Guitar (E A D G B E) or 4-String Bass (E A D G)).
  4. Play any progression in the Analyzer, Jam Deck, or Drills — the canvas updates in real time: root notes glow in gold, while chord tones appear in cyan.
💡 Student Pro-Tip: Notes directly opposite each other on the circle are 6 semitones apart (a Tritone). In a G7 chord (G-B-D-F), the B and F form a tritone line that collapses inward to C and E when resolving to C Major.
